# Hong Kong Subsidiary Architecture

Use this reference when the user asks about setting up a Hong Kong subsidiary to handle Tencent Cloud Hong Kong, Cloudflare, Stripe income, overseas product income such as Waffo Pancake, or lawful profit return to a Shanghai parent.

## Recommended framing

When the facts support it, position the entities like this:

- Hong Kong subsidiary = overseas operating entity
- Shanghai parent = R&D, support, operations, or shared service center

This framing is usually more persuasive than treating Hong Kong as a pure payment shell.

## Revenue and cost alignment

If the user mentions Stripe or product income:

- Explain that overseas revenue can sit in the Hong Kong subsidiary when:
  - customer terms or contracts name the Hong Kong entity
  - website legal pages identify the Hong Kong entity
  - Stripe merchant account belongs to the Hong Kong entity
  - settlement bank account belongs to the Hong Kong entity
  - the Hong Kong entity bears customer-facing responsibility

If the user mentions Tencent Cloud Hong Kong or Cloudflare costs:

- Explain that these costs can sit in the Hong Kong subsidiary when it is the real contracting party or service user.
- Encourage alignment across:
  - billing entity
  - payment method
  - invoice recipient
  - business purpose

## Core architecture checks

Always mention the two biggest checks:

### 1. Hong Kong substance

The Hong Kong subsidiary should ideally have:

- its own bank account
- bookkeeping and annual audit
- real board or management decisions
- ability to sign contracts and receive customer payments
- some personnel or outsourced support that matches the business story

### 2. Transfer pricing

If Shanghai performs substantial development, operations, analytics, or support:

- Shanghai should be compensated reasonably.
- Do not describe a structure where most profit stays in Hong Kong while Shanghai only absorbs costs with little or no reward.

## Profit return to Shanghai

When the user asks how profits can be returned lawfully:

- Prefer this explanation:
  - Hong Kong subsidiary earns overseas revenue and bears overseas costs.
  - Shanghai parent charges reasonable intercompany service fees for actual work performed.
  - Remaining after-tax profit in Hong Kong can later be distributed as dividends to the Shanghai parent.

Also explain:

- dividends are often easier to explain than royalties or aggressive IP charges
- the exact PRC tax impact, ODI handling, banking documents, and foreign tax credit position require professional confirmation

## Suggested checklist to offer

When the user wants a practical structure checklist, offer:

- entity roles and business scope
- customer contract party
- Stripe merchant and settlement bank account
- Tencent Cloud Hong Kong and Cloudflare billing entity
- intercompany service agreement
- transfer pricing support
- Hong Kong bookkeeping, audit, and tax filings
- mainland ODI, bank, and dividend remittance review

## Response style

Use plain, practical wording. Prefer:

- `这条路原则上可行，但关键是业务、合同、资金流和税务处理要对得上`
- `最稳的路径通常是部分服务费加税后分红`
- `要避免让香港公司看起来只是过账壳公司`
